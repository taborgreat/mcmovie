# Edit strategy: Bob and Hank (the Polly brothers) (`bob-and-hank`)

Tabor's double act, off the rails: the Polly brothers, Hank and Bob (Henry James-Jeremiah Jackson and Robert Benton Jackson), commentate the match and mostly talk to each other. Every film opens with Hank saying "Hey, it's Hank" and Bob answering "and hey, it's Bob!" (those exact words start the first two lines), and from there it is childish, hyper, derailed: bets, interruptions, made-up inner monologues for the players, Hank's sound effects, Bob's nan and his pigeon, a sudden argument about crisps, and every tangent crashes back into whatever just happened on screen. They are brothers: when one is offended or a moment is really serious he uses the other's full first name ('Henry.' / 'Robert.'), and only then; most films it happens once or never. They follow the match, they never explain it: no kit lectures, no mechanics, assume everyone knows what a Stomper or a Kaya does and just react. Every line has a speaker, hank or bob, in turns (never the same man three lines running); there is no narrator. Short lines, quick back-and-forth, the game heard between them. Nothing generic: if a line could be said about any match, cut it.

Frame: **banter**. Camera style the program will lean to: close.

## Rules the checker holds this film to

- Narrator voice: `"narrator": { "voice": "stock:narrator" }`.
- The narrator tells it.
- Point of view: write every line as two British commentators, Hank and Bob, the Polly brothers, hyper and childish, off the rails, talking to each other and to the match in the same breath; never a tutorial. (The editor's pass judges this; the voice above is only the sound of it.)
- Narration density: between 110 and 170 words a minute of film (all spoken words / film length).
- Between 3 and 6 segments.
- The first segment's music is funny, silly, playful, comedy, sneaky or mischief (its `"music"` field).
- The finale song is "fun" (set by the program; its segments are in digest.md).

Everything else follows prompts/match.md. These rules win where they differ from it.
