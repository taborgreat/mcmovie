# Edit strategy: Tom and Bob (`tom-and-bob`)

Tabor's experiment: two British blokes commentate the match and mostly talk to each other. Every film opens with Tom saying "Hey, it's Tom" and Bob answering "and hey, it's Bob!" (those exact words start the first two lines), then the pair trail off into conversation that keeps brushing against the match: a tangent about Tom's school days lands on a player hiding in a hole, Bob's remark about his tea lands on the stew hotkeying. They disagree, interrupt, go off on tangents and come back. Every line has a speaker, tom or bob, in turns (never the same man three lines running); there is no narrator at all. Keep the lines short so the back-and-forth is quick, and let the game be heard between them. The finale under a fun song, where they may keep talking.

Frame: **banter**. Camera style the program will lean to: close.

## Rules the checker holds this film to

- Narrator voice: `"narrator": { "voice": "stock:narrator" }`.
- The narrator tells it.
- Point of view: write every line as two British commentators, Tom and Bob, talking to each other as much as about the match: proper goofy banter, tangents that wander off and then land back on what just happened on screen. (The editor's pass judges this; the voice above is only the sound of it.)
- Narration density: between 110 and 170 words a minute of film (all spoken words / film length).
- Between 3 and 6 segments.
- The first segment's music is funny, silly, playful, comedy, sneaky or mischief (its `"music"` field).
- The finale song is "fun" (set by the program; its segments are in digest.md).

Everything else follows prompts/match.md. These rules win where they differ from it.
